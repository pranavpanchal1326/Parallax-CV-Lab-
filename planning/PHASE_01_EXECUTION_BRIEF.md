## 3. Product definition

PARALLAX is a proposed lab operating experience connecting learning, equipment, space, time, and evidence.
It supports the journey from experiment preparation to reproducible submission.
Its physical plan, signage, digital map, and equipment records share identifiers.
Its visual expression derives from perception, sampling, camera geometry, and scientific observation.

The assignment's required product is a design proposal and interactive prototype.
A functioning web demonstrator can strengthen selected interactions.
A production backend, live hardware gateway, institutional SSO, and AI service are conditional later integrations.
Designing their states remains within scope even when the prototype uses labelled fixtures.

## 6. Blueprint findings and corrections

### F01 Phase numbering differed from the agreed execution plan

The master blueprint originally listed Phase 0 through Phase 8.
The current plan uses ten phases.
Synchronise the master scope section to the ten-phase plan and retain its page/component references.

### F02 Attendance was implicit

P11 check-in contributes attendance, but a faculty roster and correction workflow need explicit design.
Add a P27 attendance view with present, absent, pending verification, and corrected states.
Record authorised correction actor, reason, and time.
The attendance model is booking/session-based.
No biometric identification is assumed.

### F03 Asset tracking was mostly equipment status

P09 identity and P25 maintenance do not by themselves explain movement.
Add P25 asset-history view with assigned location, recorded move, return, and service events.
Use a stable asset ID, previous/new location, actor, timestamp, and reason.
A digital location record does not prove live physical location.
Do not claim sensor-based tracking without a working integration.

### F04 Proposed palette was too easy to mistake for a final decision

Retain token values as candidates.
Phase 3 must compare three real visual studies using the same screen content.
Freeze selected visual rules after review.
Do not expand all screens using an untested first palette.

### F05 3D was planned, but its production path was unverified

Blender executable is now confirmed.
A later small export-and-viewer test still needs to verify materials, scale, and interaction.
Do not use installation as evidence that a render or model has been produced.

### F06 Length was verified, design quality was not

The master blueprint is 5,073 lines at the start of this phase.

## 8. Core demonstration contract

### Vision journey

Use E06 object detection under lighting as the initial example.
Prepare an object-only scene with a camera and controllable light.
Show experiment preparation and required-kit reservation.
Show check-in, capture settings, and labelled simulated processing if necessary.
Compare outputs and expose missed detections or ambiguous results.
Save an observation with source and parameter context.
Submit the evidence manifest.

### Signal journey

Compare E01 sampling/aliasing and E02 noise filtering in Phase 2.
Select the one that best matches curriculum, equipment, and understandable evidence.
Provide waveform and frequency-domain views.
Show sample rate or filter parameters with units.
Retain original input and processing settings.
Document a tradeoff rather than inventing improvement claims.

### Spatial learning

Use E12 Scene Studio to show camera viewpoint and occlusion.
Link the 3D scene, camera view, and explanation.
Provide prepared static comparisons for unsupported devices.
Label simulation visibly.

### Operational exception

Report a camera-kit fault through P23.
Show the issue in P25.
Apply a demonstrated restriction that affects P06 and P09.
Offer another valid time or compatible setup.
Record verification before restoring readiness.

## 11. Quality protection system

### Baseline

Artistic direction is a requirement.
Task comprehension and scientific accuracy are also requirements.
A visual page is judged with realistic content, not a sparse perfect-looking fixture.

### Representative-screen gate

Before broad high-fidelity production, compare entry, booking, workbench, and results.
These cover expression, scheduling density, instrument controls, and evidence interpretation.
Include mobile views and at least one failure state.
Use the same content across all three visual directions.

### Repeatable review

For each page, record:
- Its user job and primary action.
- Its composition rationale.
- Its realistic data and long-content case.
- Its relevant empty, loading, error, selected, and permission states.
- Its mobile adaptation.
- Its keyboard and non-3D alternative.
- Its relationship to neighbouring tasks.
- Its outstanding verification.

### Rework triggers

- A page looks unrelated to the selected visual system.
- Decorative elements hide labels, controls, or evidence.
- A key task has a dead end.
- A state contradicts a connected record.
- A 3D scene becomes the only usable route.
- A figure omits units or processing context.
- A page loses essential information at narrow widths.
- A model or render disagrees with the plan.

Resolve critical failures before expanding or calling the relevant phase complete.
Keep lower-impact findings in a visible revision log.
