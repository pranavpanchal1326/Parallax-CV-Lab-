# PARALLAX — secondary-research methodology and scope

U02-R2 · 3 October 2026 · **U02 complete within the agreed desk-research scope.**

The user has explicitly chosen a secondary-research-only project. No local interviews, surveys, participant usability sessions or field observations will be conducted. They are excluded activities, not overdue deliverables. This decision changes the project's execution scope; it does not change the assignment's marking scheme.

## Method used

1. **Focused public evidence review:** fourteen sources covering experimental reasoning, preparation, provenance, facility operations, accessibility, ventilation, sustainability and AI tutoring. The register retains dates, reading depth, population/context and access limitations.
2. **Critical synthesis:** organise evidence into six design tensions and distinguish a published finding, an external policy/standard, a public anecdote and our own design inference. Frequency and prevalence in this lab remain unknown.
3. **Evidence-to-design mapping:** connect the six problem statements, twelve local assumptions and twenty provisional requirements to source IDs, eight decisions and fifteen wireframes. Where no source directly supports a local claim, record the gap rather than adding a convenient citation.
4. **Project-artifact analysis:** inspect existing method fixtures, Figma exports, domain verification, geometry and rendered review pages. These establish what the prototype shows and how its specified rules behave; they are not observations of student behaviour.
5. **Designer-led walkthrough:** review the designed route and failure states against stated tasks, source/provenance boundaries, recoverability and access alternatives. A walkthrough can expose structural/copy problems; it cannot establish ease of use, user preference or learning improvement.
6. **Technical verification:** validate document references, coverage, preservation and rendered browser behaviour. Report these separately from published research and designer judgment.

## Evidence labels

| Label | Permitted claim | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Published study | A finding reported in the named population and study context | No transfer of effect size or local prevalence |
| Institutional policy | How the named external facility operates | Not a verified policy for PARALLAX |
| Standard/guidance | A design input or evaluation criterion | Not a certificate for the whole prototype or room |
| Public discussion | A reported experience, contradiction or question worth considering | Not representative, verified or local fieldwork |
| Design inference | A reasoned choice derived from evidence and project constraints | Not a participant-validated finding |
| Proto-persona / assumption | An authored model used to organise design decisions | Not a researched biography or quotation |
| Designer walkthrough | A structural/interface finding from the project artifacts | Not a participant usability outcome |
| Script/browser check | A verified behaviour within the stated technical scope | Not real equipment performance or learning evidence |

All six role personas, empathy prompts and journey maps use the assumption/design-inference labels. H01–H12 remain **unvalidated local assumptions**. Their uncertainty is recorded as a limitation; it does not block completion of this agreed methodology. No participant notes, consent, recruitment counts or survey findings are fabricated. The collection pack and original participant plan are retained as excluded historical material.

## Report-ready methodology statement

> This project uses focused secondary research and design-artifact analysis to inform the PARALLAX smart-lab experience. Fourteen published research, institutional, standards/guidance and public discussion sources were reviewed with explicit access and context limitations. The synthesis informed six design tensions, six assumption-based role personas, twelve local assumptions, twenty provisional requirements and eight design decisions. Fifteen wireframes and an illustrated storyboard communicate the resulting experience. Designer-led walkthroughs and technical/browser checks support structural and implementation review. No local interviews, surveys, participant usability study or site observation was conducted. Local user behaviour, usability, learning benefits and physical conditions therefore remain unverified and are reported as limitations.
